TA FEEDBACK TO STUDENT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Strong work overall. The fundamentals are clearly understood — your gradient
derivations are correct, both gradient-descent variants work, and you addressed
all the mandatory deliverables for both notebooks. The SageMaker evidence is
exemplary.

Two concrete improvements for future work:

1. When running learning-rate experiments, always plot the full convergence
   curve for each rate on the same axes. This makes it immediately visible how
   divergence, slow convergence, and good convergence differ visually — which
   is the whole pedagogical point of the exercise.

2. In multi-feature regression, standardize your features before training
   (subtract mean, divide by standard deviation). When feature scales differ
   by three orders of magnitude (T ~ 5000 vs. M ~ 1.0), gradient descent
   becomes unstable unless the learning rate is reduced to near-zero. Feature
   scaling fixes this, allows a reasonable alpha, and dramatically speeds up
   convergence. Note this in your notebook when you choose an unusual alpha.

These are standard professional practices, not just style preferences. Adding
them would push this submission to a near-perfect score.
